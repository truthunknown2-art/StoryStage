# Directing grammar

## Beat ladder

Build causality before coverage:

```text
setup → pursuit/question → threshold → discovery → misread → consequence → reinterpretation → payoff
```

Not every story needs every label, but every beat must change audience knowledge, emotion, or expectation. A shot is justified only when it helps create or read that change.

For each beat answer:

- What does the audience look for?
- Where is attention at the start and end?
- What action carries the change?
- Whose reaction proves the change landed?
- What visual information must survive with sound muted?
- What audio information must survive with picture hidden?

## Kids Adventure

- Establish geography before fast movement or a threat.
- Favor clear action → reaction → consequence chains.
- Hold long enough to read anticipation, action, and settle; cut once the new idea lands.
- Motivate camera movement with a subject, discovery, or emotional shift.
- Use foreground depth and occlusion without hiding faces or contacts at the important instant.
- Preserve friendly silhouettes, expressive asymmetry, readable gaze, and distinct timing per child.
- Use suspense through withheld information, not muddy staging.
- Let comedy breathe: anticipation, impact, delayed reaction, embarrassed or relieved settle.
- End on a comprehension image that works without dialogue.

## Weird History editorial

- Open with a strong claim, contradiction, or question.
- Change the visual idea frequently, but keep each evidence item readable.
- Alternate presenter, archival/stock evidence, generated reconstruction, diagram, insert, and kinetic type according to meaning.
- Treat provenance as part of direction. Label reconstructions and distinguish evidence from illustration.
- Use text for one short idea at a time; do not subtitle the entire narration as decoration.
- Cut on clauses, reversals, evidence reveals, punch lines, and conceptual pivots.
- Use camera moves sparingly; editorial replacement is usually stronger than constant drifting.
- Return to a recognizable presenter or graphic frame after dense evidence so the audience can reset.

## Composition and continuity

- Declare focal subject, secondary subject, negative space, horizon, and depth planes.
- Track screen direction for entrances, exits, pursuits, looks, throws, and moving props.
- Preserve the 180-degree relationship unless a motivated neutral or crossing shot resets it.
- Avoid near-identical framing changes; use a meaningful scale or angle difference.
- Match action through cuts at a readable phase and preserve prop ownership and hand.
- Cut away only after the audience has registered the causal event.
- Make eyelines converge on the same screen-space target.
- Use visible foreground objects as occluders; never simulate depth with unexplained clipping.

## Shot motivation test

Reject or merge a shot if none apply:

- introduces geography;
- advances physical action;
- reveals information;
- shows a necessary reaction;
- changes emotional distance;
- clarifies evidence or a prop;
- creates or releases tension;
- lands a joke or payoff;
- bridges time or location with an intentional transition.
